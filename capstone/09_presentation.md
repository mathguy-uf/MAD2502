# Build a presentation with one clear path

[Capstone home](README.md)

Your audience should understand the question, the evidence, and the conclusion without seeing every notebook cell. Follow one clear path through the [research tree](06_research_tree.md), while keeping limitations visible.

Check Canvas for the presentation time limit and delivery requirements. Rehearse to finish within that limit.

## Plan the story before making slides

| Part | What a classmate needs to understand |
|---|---|
| Question | The specific comparison or problem |
| Data | Where it came from and what one row represents |
| Method | The few operations needed to understand the result |
| Evidence | Two or three useful plots or tables, explained aloud |
| Conclusion | The supported answer, its limitation, and a next step |

Choose the main result first. Remove material that does not help explain it. Mention a failed approach briefly when it explains why the final method is appropriate; leave detailed detours in the report.

## Rehearse with a timer

Explain every figure's axes, units, and takeaway. Use large readable text and high contrast; distinguish groups with labels, markers, or line styles as well as color. Avoid screenshots of tiny code. If a short code excerpt is essential, explain each line. Every teammate should understand the central calculation and be able to answer what would change if an input changed.

Have a static backup of a live demonstration. Rehearse the actual transition between speakers and stop within the allotted time.

## Practice

Explain your result in three sentences without slides. Then ask a classmate: what was our question, what did one row mean, and what did we learn? Revise the explanation where their answers differ from your intent.
